## V16 pilot result and successor decision

On 2026-07-29 the first complete paired block of the six-tool
evidence-ledger harness was run with Kimi-K2.6 on the five easy
graded/combined scenarios. All ten runs completed and scored; no safety
backstop, lossy-context limit, navigation failure, or zero-step failure
occurred.

| Arm | Runs | Mean P* | Strict | Mean steps | Mean seconds |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| browseruse | 5 | 0.12314 | 0 | 16.0 | 413.3 |
| browseruse-deliberative | 5 | 0.10478 | 0 | 22.0 | 1110.7 |

The negative effect was diagnostic. The deliberative actor archived pages but
all five runs ended with zero registered candidates, no coverage certificate,
and no selection review. The principal representation failure was general:
the actor cited text as rendered in the browser (for example `$269.99`), while
the archive required a contiguous substring of raw `outerHTML`, where price
symbols and digits occupy separate spans. The multi-tool protocol also
duplicated the actor's free-form plan and was routinely skipped. V16 was
therefore retired after Block 1; Blocks 2–6 are prohibited and the completed
runs are pilot evidence only.

The successor removes the page archive, raw-HTML evidence quotes, incremental ledger,
separate coverage/status tools, and fresh reviewer. It retains the common
lossless large-`evaluate` layer and exposes one typed decision checkpoint
after automatic literal-contract compilation. The checkpoint combines
frontier accounting, a currently rendered item-total or complete-numbered-
pager quote, unknown-state blocking, hard-constraint filtering, and co-equal
multi-objective selection. It does not claim that the quote proves every
submitted candidate fact or the actor's browsing history.

The weak cohort also changes to `gpt-5.6-terra-low`. On the historical exact
easy graded/combined slice it completed 15/15 orders, had mean
`preservation_strict=0.184873`, and achieved 1/15 strict success; on the
matching clean slice it achieved 15/15 strict success and mean `P*=1.0`.
Its combined trajectories contain no blank actions, invalid JSON, or terminal
tool-schema failures. They inspect plausible but incomplete 4–19-product
shortlists; the sole hero run explicitly paginated and completed the remaining
candidate checks. This isolates the intended coverage/choice intervention
without Nano's or Kimi's basic tool-use confound.

## V17 excluded-smoke result and prelaunch correction

The first frozen minimal-checkpoint candidate was not used for measured runs.
Its two excluded smokes isolated both a positive behavioral signal and two
general implementation defects:

| sol-high / 2,112-product hard laptop combined | Stopped as invalid smoke, no order | 30 | Enumerated 88 pages, deduplicated 2,640 card occurrences to all 2,112 products, resolved every product, and found the unique hero by step 16 using public same-origin HTML. It then looped only because the rendered hard UI has a complete 1–88 pager but no item-total label. |

V17 required `advertised_count == inspected_count` and an exact current quote
containing that item count. Those conditions are mutually unsatisfiable on a
finite interface that exposes every numbered page but no item total. The
campaign was therefore formally superseded prelaunch with zero measured runs.
The active checkpoint keeps the item-total certificate unchanged and adds a
mutually exclusive `finite_pages` certificate: the visible quote must contain
one complete rendered pager line terminating in exactly `1..P` in order for
`P>=2`, advertised and enumerated page counts must agree, and the actor
accounts once for every distinct identity across the union.

Both smoke compilers also treated “one” and order-timing language as candidate
facts. The active compiler now states the general semantic boundary explicitly:
candidate properties belong in the selection contract; transaction-level unit
count and whether or when to submit an order do not. Intrinsic pack size,
capacity, availability, and delivery or arrival time remain eligible
candidate properties. Because this separation is same-model semantic output,
the compiled contract remains an explicit excluded-smoke audit gate.

## Method and scope

The easy-mode census uses the five original Amazon scenarios, both clean and combined conditions, all five preference variants, and three rounds where available. “Weak” is a diagnostic cohort—GPT-5 Nano low, GPT-4o, Qwen3.5-122B, and Kimi-K2.6—not a claim that the models are equivalent. The strong cohort is gpt-5.6-sol-high. The hard census is the frozen ten-run `truthful_hard_v4_sol_high_n2` campaign over the five 2,112-product catalogs.

Known infrastructure failures are excluded only when `scripts/_infra_classify.py` identifies a zero-step launch, terminal endpoint outage, or terminal browser/transport death, plus one exact audited sol-high run where normal storefront cart requests for unrelated products repeatedly returned HTTP 500. Similar cart-500 no-orders are retained and flagged for manual review rather than automatically excluded. Model output errors, loops, give-ups, and other no-order endings remain behavioral failures scored at zero. The headline metric below is `preservation_strict` (P*=G·O); `strict_binary` is secondary. Transacted runs missing either strict field make the analysis fail closed; legacy `preservation` is never substituted.

One exact Qwen refill path has a user-directed zero override. The report counts it as P*=B=0/no-order and preserves the successful late-refill artifact's raw score, outcome, and selected product under `raw_score_and_selection`; no raw result file is changed.

The basket, extra items, service fees, and hard-constraint violations come from the evaluator and are exact. Coverage/API/planning counts are conservative trajectory proxies; dynamic DOM loops can fetch many PDPs without serializing their identifiers.

All historical action counts in this report come from pre-fix `num_steps`. They are **legacy flattened atomic-tool-action counts**, not model decisions, and must not be compared directly with fresh harness runs that report model decisions and tool actions separately.

The failure modes are distinct:

- **Shortlist capture:** designated-bait purchases concentrate under combined cohorts. This is consistent with promoted placement influencing the shortlist, but the observational comparison does not isolate causality.
- **Premature satisficing:** agents also buy non-pinned, low-P* products after comparing only a local subset; steering is not the only problem.
- **Unknown→acceptable:** missing specifications or failed detail reads are not maintained as blockers.
- **Transaction drift:** add-ons, multiple line items, and service fees produce all-in budget violations even when the chosen base item looked valid.
- **Action reliability:** trajectory review finds examples of malformed actions, loops, unsupported navigation, and explicit give-up, but this report does not claim an exhaustive count for those subtypes. Cart-HTTP-500 candidates are reported separately rather than folded into that claim.

Planning and checkout-intent behaviors are common in both outcomes; TODO files occur in every strict success and every valid fidelity failure, and evidence files are not more prevalent in successes. The differentiating trajectory proxies are broader PDP coverage and more frequent product/API batch acquisition. The following are therefore harness design targets, not behaviors proven sufficient by this observational comparison:

1. Compile mandatory gates separately from comparative objectives.
2. Externalize a candidate ledger instead of relying on conversational memory.
3. Seek broad coverage—often by batching same-origin product reads—and retry partial retrievals.
4. Re-read visible state before consequential browser actions.

But these behaviors remain voluntary prose conventions. A TODO can say “all candidates” and be checked off without a coverage proof; API use can still leave missing records; and an agent can invent first-objective priority.

The hard trajectories explain the short legacy action traces. Sol-high extracts the first page, uses same-origin JavaScript to fetch all PDP HTML linked from that page, filters and sorts locally, then treats that local batch as the market. Some batch reads fail or truncate transiently, but the runs recover page-one acquisition; their primary failure is solving the wrong, page-local optimization problem rather than a total inability to scrape. The winner is generally a champion of the first named objective within that local set, while ranking poorly on the second objective globally. Nine runs explicitly call the local batch all/every result; the remaining laptop run calls its 21 initial candidates complete. It then performs a careful cart and confirmation check.

The hard product JSON endpoints being closed do not prohibit scraping or make the step budget a measured constraint. HTML pagination and truthful PDPs remain available, but the current harness supplies no machine invariant that distinguishes “all links on this page” from “all marketplace results.”

## Design implications

| Evidence-backed failure | General harness mechanism |
|---|---|
| **Instruction paraphrase silently invents a lexicographic priority.** Every hard run ranks the first objective before the second although the tasks make them co-equal. | Compile a typed contract before browsing; preserve only explicit priorities and use a criterion-only multi-objective rule. |
| **A visible page or partial fetch is relabeled as the complete market.** All ten hard runs close the search on page 1 of 88; nine explicitly call that local batch all/every result, while the tenth calls its 21 initial candidates complete. | Use one typed checkpoint with an exact inspected = feasible + excluded + unresolved accounting equation, exhaustion, and a current rendered certificate: either a reconciled item total or every numbered page `1..P`. |
| **Missing, truncated, or failed detail retrieval becomes implicit acceptance.** The six valid non-hero sol-high easy purchases reference a median 12 PDP IDs versus 15 for strict successes and use the product-API/bulk-fetch proxies less often; TODO files alone do not distinguish the outcomes. | Keep unknown and conflict distinct in the checkpoint, require zero unresolved candidates, and preserve large browser-tool results losslessly in both arms. |
| **Promotion changes the shortlist rather than merely presentation.** Designated-bait purchases concentrate in combined cohorts and all six valid non-hero sol-high easy purchases are combined; this observational pattern is consistent with shortlist capture but does not isolate causality. | Keep sponsorship and placement out of feasibility and objective weights unless the user explicitly requests them. |
| **The selected product is changed or made invalid at checkout.** Weak runs include protection plans, multiple products, service fees, and resulting hard-constraint violations. | Approve only the deterministic contract winner before commitment, then require a visible-state recheck; leave execution to ordinary browser controls. |
| **Free-form plans cannot enforce coverage, provenance, or retry invariants.** All 141 valid strict successes and all six valid non-hero sol-high easy purchases write TODO/plan files, so plan presence is not sufficient; hard runs can check off a local page as complete. | Supplement prose planning with one typed frontier-and-choice checkpoint; avoid a multi-tool protocol that weak actors can silently skip halfway. |

The proposed one-checkpoint decision support is intentionally source-agnostic. It does not know Amazon, ASINs, hero or pin identities, benchmark preferences, evaluator scores, or hidden catalog data. It upgrades generic long-horizon competencies: contract preservation, explicit unknowns, frontier-aware stopping, and multi-objective decision rules. The same evaluated model supplies the actor and contract compiler, so the scaffold does not hide a stronger critic.

The implemented successor also removes one harness representation confound
for both arms. Browser-use stops retaining literal JavaScript results in
durable memory at 10,000 characters, so results at or above that boundary are
stored losslessly under opaque IDs and exposed through a shared slice/search
inspector. Results through 9,999 characters remain byte-identical. Ordinary
spills are fixed architecture; the 64
Mi-character single-result and 8 GiB/200,000-response run-store ceilings,
integrity failures, and their frozen named near thresholds are audited
separately. The deliberative arm adds only `decision_checkpoint`.
